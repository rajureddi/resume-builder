# ATS layout choices

Research checked on 23 November 2026 before refining the requested templates.

- [UC San Diego Career Center resume guide](https://careers.ucsd.edu/resources/guides/resume.html): simple formatting, consistent right-aligned dates, body text of at least 10 pt, relevant skills grouped into subsections, and a short job-specific summary. Its student example places education after the summary.
- [Virginia Tech ATS guidance](https://career.vt.edu/resources/applicant-tracking-systems-ats-friendly-resumes/): standard headings, a single main column, readable contact information in the document body, visible URLs, and checking the exported file for shifted formatting and unexpected pages. It recommends avoiding decorative graphics and complex columns.
- [Harvard's official resume template](https://cdn-careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/161/2024/07/resume-paragraph.pdf): a restrained academic layout, consistent education/experience alignment, and explicitly labelled skills. The supplied official template is a one-page document.

No layout guarantees acceptance by every ATS. For this project, **ATS Standard** is the recommended starting point: readable sans-serif text, a single main column, standard headings, grouped skill rows, visible contact links, and no outer border. **Classic Academic** uses serif typography and puts education near the top. **Modern Professional** uses a left-aligned header and the same thin section dividers as the other templates.

The owner's reference images request contact symbols and a compact technical section. Both are selectable: contact icons remain supplementary to the actual text, and skills can use grouped rows or two compact columns. The skills editor controls which items appear, their categories, and optional interests. Thin section dividers can also be disabled.

All layouts share actual font measurements, text wrapping, page breaks, positions, and content between the screen preview, print view, and downloaded PDF. Long content flows to additional pages rather than being omitted. Existing resumes retain their details through profile snapshots or their original stored text.
